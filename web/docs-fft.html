Spectral-Domain OCT in one A-scan

A broadband spectrum reflected by the keyhole is captured by a line-sensor. The depth information is encoded in the spectrum's oscillation frequency: a reflector at depth z adds a cos(2·k·z) ripple to the spectrum (k = wavenumber).

The Fourier transform turns oscillation frequency → depth. So the core of an SD-OCT system is one real-to-complex FFT per A-scan:

  1. Background subtraction — remove fixed-pattern DC.
  2. k-resampling — spectrometer pixels are ~linear in wavelength; the FFT needs linear-in-k (performed once per dedicated device via a calibration curve; WeldScope keeps the (linear) grid pluggable).
  3. Spectral shaping — a Hann window suppresses sidelobes (shown live below).
  4. Zero-padding to next power of two (2048 → 4096) for finer depth sampling.
  5. R2C FFT (realfft) — one complex depth profile per A-scan; magnitude → log-scaled power = the A-scan.
